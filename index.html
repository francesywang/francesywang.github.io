Political Science · International Relations

Frances Yaping Wang

Assistant Professor of Political Science · Colgate University

I study international security, authoritarian media and public opinion during interstate conflicts, and Chinese foreign policy.

My book, The Art of State Persuasion: China’s Strategic Use of Media in Interstate Disputes (Oxford University Press), examines how China uses state media as a tool of foreign policy during international conflicts. It received the International Studies Association Asia-Pacific Best Book Award and an Honorable Mention for the ISA International Security Studies Section Best Book Award.

My research has appeared in Security Studies, International Studies Quarterly, and the Journal of Contemporary China, as well as public outlets including The Diplomat, South China Morning Post, and The Independent.

Research

International Security

Conflict behavior, interstate crises, low-intensity coercion, signaling, escalation and restraint.

Media, Propaganda & Public Opinion

How governments use censorship, mobilization, delegitimization, posturing, and other communication strategies to shape domestic audiences.

Chinese Foreign Policy

China’s crisis behavior, territorial disputes, maritime conflict, and the domestic politics of external relations.

Methods

Archival research, elite interviews, computational text analysis, original datasets, and survey experiments across multiple countries.

Background

I received my Ph.D. in Politics from the University of Virginia in 2018. Before joining Colgate, I was an Assistant Professor at Singapore Management University, a postdoctoral fellow at the University of Notre Dame’s International Security Center, a Minerva–United States Institute of Peace Peace Scholar, a predoctoral fellow at the Institute for Security and Conflict Studies at George Washington University, and a senior editor at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.
